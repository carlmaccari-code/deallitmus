Glossary

Real estate investing terms, in plain English

Every term comes with its formula and a worked example.

1% rule
The 1% rule is a quick rental screen: monthly rent should be at least 1% of the purchase price for a property to deserve a closer look.
50% rule
The 50% rule estimates that about half of a rental's rent goes to operating expenses, not counting the mortgage payment.
70% rule
The 70% rule says a house flipper should pay no more than 70% of the after-repair value minus the cost of repairs.
After-repair value (ARV)
After-repair value (ARV) is the estimated market value of a property once planned repairs are done, based on sales of similar renovated homes.
Amortization
Amortization is paying off a loan in regular payments, each split between interest and principal, until the balance reaches zero.
Appreciation
Appreciation is the increase in a property’s value over time, from market forces or from improvements you make.
Assignment fee
An assignment fee is what a wholesaler is paid for transferring a purchase contract on a property to an end buyer.
Break-even occupancy
Break-even occupancy is the share of potential income a property must collect to cover its operating expenses and loan payments.
BRRRR method
BRRRR means buy, rehab, rent, refinance, repeat: a strategy that pulls most of your cash back out of one rental to fund the next.
Cap rate
Cap rate is a property's net operating income divided by its price plus any up-front repairs: the yearly pre-tax return if you paid all cash.
Capital expenditures (CapEx)
Capital expenditures (CapEx) are large, infrequent costs to replace or upgrade major parts of a property, such as a roof or furnace.
Cash flow
Cash flow is the pre-tax money a rental property has left each month or year after vacancy, operating expenses, reserves and loan payments.
Cash-on-cash return
Cash-on-cash return is a rental's annual pre-tax cash flow divided by the total cash you invested to buy it.
Cash-out refinance
A cash-out refinance replaces your mortgage with a larger loan and pays you the difference in cash, turning equity into money you can use.
Closing costs
Closing costs are the lender, title and other fees plus prepaid items you pay to complete a real estate purchase, sale or refinance.
Debt service
Debt service is the total of the loan payments, principal plus interest, that a property owner owes a lender over a period, usually a year.
Debt service coverage ratio (DSCR)
DSCR is a property's net operating income divided by its annual loan payments, showing how easily the income covers the debt.
Effective gross income
Effective gross income is the potential rent of a rental property minus vacancy and unpaid rent, plus other income such as laundry or parking.
Equity multiple
Equity multiple is the total cash an investment returns divided by the total cash invested, showing how many times you got your money back.
Gross rent multiplier (GRM)
Gross rent multiplier (GRM) is a property's price divided by its annual gross rent, a quick way to compare rentals before expenses.
Hard money loan
A hard money loan is a short-term, higher-cost loan from a private lender, secured by the property and often used for flips and rehabs.
Holding costs
Holding costs are the interest, taxes, insurance and utilities you pay to own a property while it is being fixed up, leased or sold.
House hacking
House hacking means living in one part of a property and renting out the rest so the rent covers some or all of your housing cost.
Internal rate of return (IRR)
IRR is the annual rate of return on a deal over the full hold, counting the size and timing of every cash flow, including the sale.
Loan points
Loan points are upfront lender fees paid at closing, where one point equals 1% of the loan amount.
Loan-to-value (LTV)
Loan-to-value (LTV) is a loan amount divided by the property's value, the ratio lenders use to cap how much you can borrow.
Maximum allowable offer (MAO)
Maximum allowable offer (MAO) is the highest price you can pay for a property and still hit your target profit after repairs and costs.
Net operating income (NOI)
Net operating income (NOI) is a property's yearly income after operating expenses, before mortgage payments and income taxes.
Operating expenses
Operating expenses are the recurring costs of running a rental, such as taxes, insurance, management and repairs, not including the mortgage.
PITI
PITI is the full monthly mortgage payment: principal, interest, property taxes and insurance.
Private mortgage insurance (PMI)
Private mortgage insurance (PMI) is a premium you pay that protects the lender when you put less than 20% down on a conventional loan.
Rent-to-price ratio
The rent-to-price ratio is the monthly rent on a property divided by its purchase price, used as a quick first screen for rental deals.
Seasoning period
A seasoning period is how long you must own a property or hold its loan before a lender will refinance it, commonly 6 to 12 months.
Turnkey property
A turnkey property is a renovated rental, often with a tenant and manager in place, sold ready to produce income from day one.
Vacancy rate
Vacancy rate is the share of a rental's potential rent lost because the unit sits empty, expressed as a percentage.
Wholesaling
Wholesaling means putting a property under contract and passing that contract to another investor for a fee, usually without owning it.
