Glossary

Appreciation

Appreciation is the increase in a property’s value over time, from market forces or from improvements you make.

Appreciation is the rise in a property’s value over time. Market appreciation comes from forces you do not control, such as local demand, incomes and interest rates. Forced appreciation comes from work you do, such as a renovation that lifts the after-repair value or, on properties valued by their income, rent increases that raise net operating income.

Appreciation rate = (current value minus purchase price) ÷ purchase price

You buy a rental for $250,000 and five years later it is worth $300,000. It has appreciated by $50,000, and $50,000 ÷ $250,000 = 20% in total. That averages 4% a year in simple terms, but with compounding it works out to about 3.7% a year. Leverage magnifies the result. If you put 20% down, or $50,000, the $50,000 gain equals 100% of your down payment, before selling costs and taxes. It cuts both ways: a 10% drop in value, or $25,000, would erase half of that down payment.

The most common mistake is counting on appreciation to rescue a deal that loses money every month. Values can fall or stay flat for years, and you only collect appreciation when you sell or refinance. Treat it as a bonus on top of cash flow, and test your numbers at a modest rate, or at zero, before you buy.
