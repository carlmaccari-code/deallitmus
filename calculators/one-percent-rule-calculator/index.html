1% rule calculator

A ten-second screen for rental listings: does a month of rent equal at least 1% of the price?

The 1% rule formula

Rent-to-price ratio = monthly rent ÷ purchase price

If the ratio is 1% or higher, the property passes. A $240,000 house needs to rent for at least $2,400 a month. If it rents for $2,150, the ratio is 0.90%, and to pass at that rent you would need to buy it for $215,000 or less.

What the rule is for

The 1% rule exists to save time. When you are scrolling through fifty listings, it tells you which five might cash flow and deserve a real analysis. It works because rent has to cover a mortgage, taxes, insurance, repairs and vacancy, and at typical costs that takes roughly 1% of the price each month.

Where it breaks down

  • Interest rates. The rule was popularized when mortgage rates were lower. At higher rates, a property can pass and still lose money every month.
  • Taxes and insurance. High-tax or high-insurance areas need well over 1% to break even.
  • Expensive markets. In many coastal cities almost nothing passes, and investors there buy for appreciation instead.
  • Condition. The rule compares rent with the price alone. If the house needs work, many investors add the rehab cost to the price before they divide.

Treat a pass as permission to run the full numbers, not as a verdict.

Questions

Is the 1% rule still realistic?

In many markets very few listings pass it today. Investors commonly relax the screen to 0.7% or 0.8% and then rely on a full cash flow analysis to decide. The rule is still useful for ranking listings against each other.

What is the 2% rule?

A stricter version: monthly rent of at least 2% of the price. Properties that pass are usually very low-priced and often come with higher repair costs, vacancy and management effort.

Should I include repairs in the price?

Many investors do. The rule itself divides rent by the purchase price alone, and so does the rent-to-price figure in the full analyzer. Adding any repairs needed to make the property rentable gives a stricter screen, since that is what you are really paying to get the rent.
