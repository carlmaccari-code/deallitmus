How rental cash flow is calculated

Every rental analysis follows the same chain, and it helps to see each link:

  1. Gross rent minus a vacancy allowance gives effective income.
  2. Effective income minus operating expenses (taxes, insurance, repairs, capital expenditures, management, HOA) gives net operating income, or NOI.
  3. NOI minus the mortgage payment gives cash flow.

The mistake that sinks most first deals is skipping step two. A property that rents for $2,400 with a $1,200 mortgage does not cash flow $1,200. After vacancy, repairs, reserves and management, a third to a half of the rent is usually gone before the lender is paid.

The four numbers that matter

  • Monthly cash flow: what is left in your account each month.
  • Cash-on-cash return: annual cash flow divided by the cash you put in. It measures how hard your down payment is working.
  • Cap rate: NOI divided by the price plus any up-front repairs. It ignores financing, so it compares properties rather than loans.
  • DSCR: NOI divided by annual mortgage payments. It tells you, and your lender, how much cushion the rent provides.

Quick screens: the 1% rule and the 50% rule

The 1% rule says monthly rent should be at least 1% of the purchase price. The 50% rule says operating expenses will run about half of the rent over time. Both are filters for deciding which listings deserve a full analysis, not substitutes for one.

Questions

What expenses should I include in a rental analysis?

Property taxes, insurance, repairs and maintenance, a reserve for capital expenditures such as roofs and HVAC, property management, HOA dues, any utilities you pay, and an allowance for vacancy. Leave the mortgage out of operating expenses; it is subtracted afterwards.

What is a good cash-on-cash return on a rental?

Many investors look for 8% to 12%, but it depends on the market and your goals. Appreciation-heavy markets often produce lower cash-on-cash returns, and buyers there accept that in exchange for expected growth in value.

Does this calculator include income taxes?

No. All figures are pre-tax. Depreciation, interest deductions and your own tax bracket change the after-tax result, so talk to a tax professional about your situation.
