Cap rate calculator

Net operating income divided by the price plus any up-front repairs. The quickest way to compare one property with another.

The cap rate formula

Cap rate = net operating income ÷ (property price + up-front repairs)

Net operating income (NOI) is a year of rent, minus vacancy, minus operating expenses: taxes, insurance, repairs, management and reserves. It does not include mortgage payments.

Up-front repairs are the work a property needs before it can earn that rent. Add their cost to the price you enter in the calculator. The full analyzer does the same with your rehab budget.

A worked example

Rent of $2,400 a month is $28,800 a year. A 5% vacancy allowance leaves $27,360. Subtract $9,000 of operating expenses and NOI is $18,360. On a $240,000 purchase that needs no work, the cap rate is $18,360 ÷ $240,000 = 7.65%.

What cap rate tells you, and what it doesn’t

Because it leaves out financing, cap rate lets you compare properties on equal terms, whatever loan you or anyone else might use. That is also its limit: it says nothing about your actual return once a mortgage is involved. For that, use cash-on-cash return.

Cap rate also works in reverse. If similar properties in an area trade at a 7% cap rate, a building with $21,000 of NOI is worth about $21,000 ÷ 0.07 = $300,000.

Questions

What is a good cap rate?

It depends on the market and the property. Lower cap rates usually reflect lower risk and stronger expected appreciation; higher cap rates usually come with more risk or slower growth. Compare against similar properties in the same area rather than a universal benchmark.

Does cap rate include the mortgage?

No. Cap rate is calculated before financing. Two buyers with different loans get the same cap rate on the same property.

Should I use the asking price or my offer price?

Use the price you expect to pay. If the property needs immediate repairs, many investors add that cost to the price so that the cap rate reflects the total investment.
