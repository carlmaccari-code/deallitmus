Rental property ROI calculator

Cash flow is only part of the return. Add loan paydown and appreciation to see the whole picture.

The three sources of return

  1. Cash flow. Rent minus operating expenses and the mortgage payment. This is the part you can spend.
  2. Loan paydown. Each payment retires a little principal, paid for by your tenant. It is small in the early years and grows every year.
  3. Appreciation. The change in the property’s value. With a 25% down payment, a 3% rise in value is a 12% gain on your down payment. That is leverage working in your favor; it works the same way in reverse.

Total return = (cash flow + loan paydown + appreciation) ÷ cash invested

How to read the result

Cash-on-cash return counts only the first source. Total return counts all three, which is why it is usually much higher. Be careful how much weight you put on it: cash flow is the only part that is predictable and spendable. Loan paydown is locked in the property until you sell or refinance, and appreciation is a forecast.

A property with strong total return and negative cash flow is a bet on appreciation that you have to fund every month.

Beyond year one

Returns change over a hold. Rents rise, the loan balance falls faster, and selling costs take a share at the end. The internal rate of return over the full hold period captures all of that in a single annual figure.

Questions

What is a good ROI on a rental property?

Many investors look for a total return above 10% to 15% a year, with positive cash flow. What counts as good depends on the risk, the effort involved and what else you could do with the money.

What appreciation rate should I assume?

Long-run US home price growth has averaged roughly 3% to 4% a year, with large differences between markets and decades. Using a conservative figure, or zero, shows whether the deal stands on its own.

Why is ROI different from cash-on-cash return?

Cash-on-cash return counts only cash flow. ROI as calculated here also counts loan paydown and appreciation. Both divide by the same cash invested.
