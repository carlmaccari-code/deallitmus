Wholesale deal calculator

Work back from what an investor will pay to what you can offer the seller, with your fee in between.

The wholesale formula

Your maximum offer = (ARV × 70%) − repairs − your fee

Your end buyer is usually a flipper who follows the 70% rule: they will pay up to 70% of the after-repair value, minus repairs. Everything you earn has to fit underneath that ceiling.

A worked example

A house will be worth $289,000 fixed up and needs $42,000 of work. An investor’s maximum price is $289,000 × 70% − $42,000 = $160,300. If you want a $10,000 assignment fee, the most you can put the property under contract for is $150,300.

Getting the inputs right

  • ARV comes from recent sales of renovated homes nearby, not from list prices or automated estimates.
  • Repairs should be what your buyer would budget. If you underestimate, experienced buyers will notice, and the deal will not close.
  • The percentage varies. In competitive markets buyers pay 75% or more; for landlords buying rentals, the number that matters is cash flow, not a percentage of ARV.

Stay inside the rules

Wholesaling is regulated differently in every state, and some require a real estate license or specific disclosures to market a property you do not own. Check your state’s rules and have an attorney review your contracts.

Questions

What is a typical wholesale assignment fee?

Fees of $5,000 to $15,000 are common, though they vary widely with the price of the property and the size of the spread. The fee has to leave enough margin for your buyer or the deal will not close.

What if my buyer is a landlord rather than a flipper?

Then the 70% rule does not apply. A landlord pays based on rent and cash flow, so analyze it as a rental to find the price at which it meets a buy-and-hold investor’s return target.

Should closing costs come out of my number?

Agree in the contract who pays which closing costs. If you are covering any, subtract them from your maximum offer along with your fee.
