The cash-on-cash formula

Cash-on-cash return = annual pre-tax cash flow ÷ total cash invested

Annual pre-tax cash flow is the rent you collect in a year minus operating expenses and mortgage payments. Total cash invested is everything that came out of your pocket to get the property rented: down payment, closing costs, loan points and any repairs you paid for in cash.

A worked example

A property rents for $2,400 a month. Operating expenses, including an allowance for vacancy, average $800, and the mortgage is $1,200. That leaves $400 a month, or $4,800 a year. If you invested $75,000 in total, the cash-on-cash return is $4,800 ÷ $75,000 = 6.4%.

Cash-on-cash vs. cap rate vs. total return

  • Cap rate ignores financing. It describes the property.
  • Cash-on-cash includes financing. It describes your deal.
  • Total return (IRR) adds loan paydown and appreciation over the years you hold. Cash-on-cash only counts the cash that reaches your account.

Questions

What is a good cash-on-cash return?

Many rental investors target 8% to 12%. Lower returns can still make sense in markets with strong appreciation, and higher targets are common for properties that need more work or carry more risk.

What counts as cash invested?

Your down payment, closing costs, loan points, inspection and appraisal fees, and any rehab paid out of pocket. Do not include money the lender put in.

Should operating expenses include the mortgage?

No. Operating expenses cover taxes, insurance, repairs, management, vacancy and reserves. The mortgage is entered separately.
