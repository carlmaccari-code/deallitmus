Investment property mortgage calculator

The full monthly payment on a rental: principal, interest, taxes, insurance and HOA.

What goes into the payment

Lenders and investors talk about PITI: principal, interest, taxes and insurance. Add HOA dues where they apply and you have the amount that leaves your account each month whether or not the property is rented.

Principal and interest come from the standard amortization formula and stay fixed for the life of a fixed-rate loan. Taxes and insurance do not; expect them to rise over time.

How investment loans differ

  • Down payment. Conventional loans on rentals usually require 15% to 25% down, compared with as little as 3% to 5% for a home you live in.
  • Rate. Expect a higher rate than on an owner-occupied loan, often by half a point to a full point.
  • Reserves. Lenders commonly want to see several months of payments in the bank.
  • DSCR loans. Some lenders qualify the property rather than you, based on whether the rent covers the payment.

The payment is not the whole cost

A rental that brings in exactly its PITI is losing money, because repairs, vacancy, capital expenditures and management come on top. To see whether the rent actually covers the property, run a full rental analysis.

Questions

How much do I need to put down on an investment property?

Conventional lenders typically require 15% to 25% for a rental property, with better rates at 25% or more. Requirements vary by lender, loan type and the number of units.

Do investment property loans have mortgage insurance?

Conventional investment loans generally require enough down payment that private mortgage insurance does not apply. Owner-occupied multi-unit purchases with low down payments are the usual exception.

What rent do I need to cover this payment?

As a rough guide, rent should exceed the full payment by enough to cover vacancy, repairs, capital expenditures and management, which together often take 30% to 50% of rent. A lender using a 1.25 DSCR will want net operating income 25% above principal and interest.
