Fix and flip calculator

Count the costs between the purchase and the sale, and see what is actually left: net profit, return on cash and your break-even price.

How flip profit is calculated

Net profit is the sale price minus everything it took to get there:

  • Purchase price and closing costs
  • Rehab
  • Financing: points up front and interest every month
  • Holding costs: property taxes and insurance while you own it. Utilities, HOA dues and other monthly carrying costs are not included here; add them in the full analyzer
  • Selling costs: agent commissions, concessions and closing costs on the way out

New flippers tend to budget the first two and discover the last three at closing. On a typical project, financing, holding and selling costs together run 10% to 15% of the sale price.

Return on cash and annualized return

Return on cash is net profit divided by the cash you actually put in. With leverage that is far less than the total project cost, which is why flip returns look high. The annualized figure scales the return to a full year so you can compare a four-month flip with an eight-month one.

Know your break-even price

The break-even sale price is the lowest price at which you walk away with nothing lost. The gap between it and your expected sale price is your cushion if the market softens or the buyer negotiates.

Questions

How much profit should a flip make?

Many flippers look for a net profit of at least 10% to 15% of the sale price, or a fixed minimum such as $25,000 to $30,000, to justify the risk and the work. Thin margins disappear quickly when a rehab runs over.

What are typical selling costs?

Commonly 6% to 8% of the sale price, covering agent commissions, title and escrow fees, transfer taxes and any buyer concessions. They vary by market.

How does this relate to the 70% rule?

The 70% rule is a shortcut for the same math: it assumes that financing, holding, selling costs and profit will take about 30% of the after-repair value. This calculator replaces that assumption with your actual numbers.
