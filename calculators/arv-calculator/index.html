ARV calculator

Estimate what a property will be worth once renovated, from recent sales of similar renovated homes.

How ARV is estimated

ARV = average price per square foot of the comps × your property’s square feet

After-repair value is what the property should sell for once the work is done. The estimate comes from comps: recently sold homes that are close by, similar in size and layout, and already in the condition yours will be in after renovation.

Choosing good comps

  • Sold, not listed. Asking prices are hopes. Use closed sales from the last three to six months.
  • Close. The same neighborhood or subdivision, ideally within half a mile, and on the same side of any major road or school boundary.
  • Similar. Within about 20% of the size, with the same number of bedrooms and bathrooms, and of a similar age and style.
  • Renovated. Comps should match your finished condition, not the current one.

Why the range matters

The calculator shows a low and a high estimate alongside the average. If your deal only works at the high end, it does not work. Experienced flippers underwrite to the conservative end of the range and treat anything above it as a bonus.

The limits of price per square foot

It is a starting point. It does not account for a garage, a finished basement, a bigger lot or a busy street. Adjust for differences like these, or better, ask a local agent for a comparative market analysis before you commit.

Questions

What does ARV stand for?

After-repair value: the estimated market value of a property once planned renovations are complete.

How many comps do I need?

Three good ones are the usual minimum. More helps if they are truly comparable, but one excellent comp from the same street is worth more than five loose ones from across town.

Can I use an online home value estimate as the ARV?

Not reliably. Automated estimates describe the home in its current, unrenovated condition and can be far off for properties that need work. Use renovated comps instead.
