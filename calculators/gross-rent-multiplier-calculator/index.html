Gross rent multiplier calculator

How many years of gross rent does the price represent? A quick way to compare similar properties.

The GRM formula

Gross rent multiplier = property price ÷ annual gross rent

A $240,000 property that rents for $2,400 a month brings in $28,800 a year. Its GRM is $240,000 ÷ $28,800 = 8.33. Lower is better: you are paying fewer dollars for each dollar of rent.

Gross yield is the same idea, upside down

Gross rental yield is annual rent divided by price. The example above yields 12%. A GRM of 10 is a 10% gross yield; a GRM of 12.5 is 8%.

How to use it

GRM is most useful for comparing similar properties in the same area, where taxes, insurance and condition are roughly alike. If comparable duplexes sell at a GRM of 9 and one is listed at 7, it is either a bargain or hiding a problem, and both are worth knowing.

You can also use it to estimate value: annual rent multiplied by the local GRM gives a rough price.

What it leaves out

Everything except price and rent. GRM ignores vacancy, expenses and financing, so two properties with the same GRM can produce very different cash flow. Cap rate brings in expenses, and cash-on-cash return brings in the loan.

Questions

What is a good gross rent multiplier?

It depends on the market. Many cash-flow investors look for a GRM under about 10, but expensive markets routinely trade at 15 or higher. Compare against similar properties nearby rather than a universal number.

What is the difference between GRM and cap rate?

GRM uses gross rent and ignores expenses. Cap rate uses net operating income, which is rent after vacancy and operating expenses. Cap rate is the more complete measure; GRM is faster.

Should I use monthly or annual rent?

The standard definition uses annual rent. Some people quote a monthly version, which is simply twelve times larger, so check which one a listing or agent is using.
